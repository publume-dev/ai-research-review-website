The study addresses separation capacity in reservoir computing, proposing that the expected separation induced by a random linear reservoir is completely characterized by the spectral properties of a positive semi-definite matrix called the generalized matrix of moments.

For symmetric Gaussian connectivity matrices, the classical N^{-1/2} scaling yields the most balanced separation for large reservoirs, but separation quality deteriorates as input length increases. For i.i.d. connectivity entries, the same scaling is asymptotically optimal. Numerical experiments suggest a strong link between balanced separation and downstream learning performance.

The paper is a preprint and has not been peer-reviewed; all theoretical results and numerical experiments are self-reported.
