The study compares the persuasiveness of large language models (LLMs) against incentivized human persuaders in a large-scale interactive experiment, covering both truthful and deceptive scenarios, with a replication experiment.

Claude 3.5 Sonnet was more persuasive than incentivized humans in both truthful and deceptive contexts, significantly increasing accuracy when persuasion was truthful and decreasing accuracy when deceptive. A replication using DeepSeek v3 confirmed the accuracy effects but found greater LLM persuasiveness only in deceptive contexts. Linguistic analyses of persuader texts suggest the effects may stem from LLMs expressing higher conviction than humans.

The paper is an arXiv preprint and has not been peer-reviewed. The abstract does not provide detailed information on experimental design, sample sizes, effect sizes, or statistical significance.
