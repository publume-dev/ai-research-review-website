Configuration tuning uses surrogate models to approximate how untried configurations perform, but an accurate predictor is not necessarily a useful one. The paper proposes a fitness-landscape-analysis-based theory that evaluates surrogate-model usefulness for tuning instead of relying on accuracy alone. The arXiv abstract states that the work has been accepted by TOSEM.

The authors also introduce Model4Tune, a predictive tool that estimates which model–tuner pairs are best for an unforeseen system without requiring expensive tuner profiling. In an empirical study of up to 27,000 cases, Model4Tune performed significantly better than random guessing in 79%–82% of cases.

Only the abstract is available for this assessment, so the detailed study design, the systems and tuners covered, the performance metrics, confidence intervals, and replication status have not been verified. The acceptance by TOSEM is noted in the abstract comment, but the arXiv posting does not include the full review history.
