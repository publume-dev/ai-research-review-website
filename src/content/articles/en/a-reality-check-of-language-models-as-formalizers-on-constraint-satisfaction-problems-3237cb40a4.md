The study provides a systematic examination of using large language models (LLMs) as formalizers, where models generate formal specifications for constraint satisfaction problems instead of directly solving them. The evaluation spans 4 benchmarks, 6 LLMs, and 2 formal languages.

The findings show that across all 24 model-dataset combinations, LLM-as-formalizer underperforms LLM-as-solver in 15 combinations. This suggests that current LLMs do not consistently outperform direct solving when generating formal constraints, and their formalization may degrade with problem complexity.

The abstract does not specify the exact benchmarks, LLMs, or formal languages used, nor the magnitude of performance differences. Thus, the scope and effect sizes of the reported finding remain unclear.
