The study compares transformer language models trained with and without fleeting memory on a developmentally realistic training set. Fleeting memory is designed to mimic human working memory and may influence how models process linguistic information.

Transformers with fleeting memory consistently outperform those without in overall language modeling and targeted syntactic evaluation. However, they perform worse in surprisal-based prediction of human reading times. This indicates that fleeting memory benefits language learning but hinders the prediction of human processing difficulty.

The abstract does not provide quantitative effect sizes or benchmark deltas, so the magnitude of the improvement or impairment is not available from the supplied evidence.
